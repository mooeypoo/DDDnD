# Community packs

A **community pack** is a set of adventures made by someone other than DDDnD: a workshop facilitator, a team retelling the game for their own company, a fan exploring a different kind of system. Each pack is its own world, with its own scenarios, stakeholders and events.

DDDnD doesn't review community packs. You decide which ones to import.

## Is it safe?

Packs are data, not programs. **A pack can't run code, reach your device, or load anything from the internet.** The worst a pack can do is show you text you didn't expect.

- Everything a pack says is shown as plain text: no formatting, no clickable links.
- The only links a pack can offer are its author's and homepage links. They show where they lead, open in a new tab, and come with a reminder that they lead to sites DDDnD doesn't know.
- Every pack is checked when it is imported. A pack that breaks the rules isn't imported, and you're told why.

## The Community Bazaar

Community packs live in their own place, apart from DDDnD's own adventures. From the council lobby, choose **Community bazaar ›**.

- The bazaar shows one stall per pack you've imported: its name, **who published it**, how many adventures it has, and what it's about. Community content always has a stitched border, which DDDnD's own content never uses.
- **Open** a stall to sit at that pack's own table. It works like the lobby: choose an adventure (or one of its tutorials, if it has any), choose your class, and join.
- During a community adventure, the results screen and the share card say **"Community pack · name · publisher"**, so a screenshot or a shared card never passes for a DDDnD scenario.

## Importing a pack

In the bazaar, choose **Import a pack**, then either:

- **paste a link** the pack's author gave you (a GitHub link to the pack's `manifest.json`), or
- **choose a file** ending in `.dddnd-pack.json`.

Before anything is imported, you see what the pack contains, who published it, its license, and whether its link is pinned to a release. Nothing is imported until you confirm.

Loading from a link downloads the pack from GitHub, which can see your IP address, as any website can. After that, nothing more is downloaded. Loading a file doesn't contact anyone.

Someone may share a link like `https://dddnd.app/community?pack=…`. It opens the import screen with the link filled in. It never imports anything by itself.

## Who published it?

A pack's name is chosen by its author, and two packs can have the same name. The game tells them apart by **where they come from**:

- **On GitHub:** the account and repository it was loaded from, for example "mooeypoo on GitHub (mooeypoo/DDDnD-packs)". The pack can't fake this.
- **From a file:** "unknown". A file can say anything about its author, so the bazaar doesn't vouch for it.

The author names a pack lists for itself are shown as credits, separately from the publisher.

## Where your packs are kept

**Packs are kept in this browser only.** DDDnD has no accounts and doesn't store anything about you. So:

- your packs don't follow you to another browser, another device, or a private window;
- clearing this site's data removes them;
- some browsers don't let sites keep anything, and then packs last only until you close the page. The bazaar tells you when that happens.

**Keep the links (or files) of packs you like somewhere of your own,** so you can import them again.

## Saved games

A game in progress is saved in your browser, together with exactly which pack it was played with.

- If you remove that pack, the saved game is kept. The bazaar explains what's missing, and the game continues once you import the same pack again.
- If the pack changes (a new release, or a different pack with the same name), the saved game can't continue with it. Saved games only continue with the exact content they were played with. You can start a new game.

## Removing a pack

Choose **Remove** on its stall. You're asked first, because a saved game from that pack can't continue until you import it again.
